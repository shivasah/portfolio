“I rebuild the same report every month.”

Every month-end, accountants export QuickBooks reports into Word, PowerPoint or Google Docs, write commentary by hand, and repeat it for every client.

Legacy Management Reports could bundle statements into a PDF, but with fixed templates, limited customization and no place to write. Customers kept the reusable part and did the rest outside QuickBooks.

Why it mattered

The report is the moment the books become advice.

Customers

VOC across Slack, support and research kept surfacing the same three asks: control over data, control over presentation, less rework.

Business

Only 2.5% of QuickBooks customers used legacy Management Reports in the project baseline. Accountants were taking the work elsewhere at the moment they needed to explain their value to clients.

Platform

QuickBooks was moving toward an AI-native, builder-based architecture. Reporting needed an authoring foundation that could carry AI assistance, not another template.

Decision 02

Bring numbers and commentary into one editor.

Dashboards answer “what’s happening”; a management report answers “what should we do”. The editor had to feel like writing, with the numbers live inside it.

The report sets the default time period.

Decision 03

Use the report period by default.

Newly inserted reports should use the management report’s period. A saved custom report can be an exception; make that difference explicit instead of silently changing its dates.

Exploration 01

Saved dates, explained after insertion

A contextual message explains why this block uses saved dates and offers “Match to management report”.

It keeps the flow moving, but the person needs to notice the mismatch after the report is already on the canvas.

Exploration 02

Choose a period while inserting

A compact insert dialog starts with the management report’s global time period.

The default is visible, but asking on every insertion adds a step even when the periods already match.

Exploration 03

Make the date choices explicit

A dialog separates keeping saved dates, matching the management report and choosing another period.

The distinction is clear. It is most useful when there is a real exception, rather than as a routine interruption.

1 of 3

A sensible default, with room for exceptions.

Keep one reporting period as the default. When saved dates differ, explain the difference and let the author keep those dates or match the report. The rule does the routine work; the author handles the exception.

What we could have done better

A better builder should not mean starting over.

Legacy users had already invested time in their reports. Seamlessly carrying those reports into the new editor would have reduced the effort of making the switch.

We could not deliver that migration because the two experiences used different architectures. Translating an existing report into the new builder was an engineering challenge, leaving customers to recreate their setup.

The missed opportunity

Bring their work with them.

Treat migration as part of onboarding, not a separate task for the customer.
